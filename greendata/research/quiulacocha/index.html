The method in brief

We collected acid mine drainage at two points (PM1 and PM2) in Quiulacocha, Pasco. In the lab, we treated it with natural bioflocculants made from pitahaya (Selenicereus undatus) and zapallo macre (Cucurbita maxima) in Jar Tests organised with a Box-Behnken experimental design.

Iron, lead, zinc, copper and chromium were measured by ICP-OES before and after treatment. A Random Forest model learned which conditions drive metal removal, and IDW interpolation in ArcGIS Pro mapped the results.

Limitations

  • Lab-scale Jar Tests: full-scale treatment may behave differently.
  • Samples come from two points on a single sampling date.
  • Results apply to this drainage's chemistry and may not transfer to other mine waters.

Credits

Undergraduate thesis at Universidad Nacional Federico Villarreal (UNFV), Faculty of Geographic, Environmental and Ecotourism Engineering, co-authored with Yajhaira and Jhilary. Jar Test experiments at the Environmental Engineering Laboratory of UTEC.

Why it matters

Acid mine drainage releases heavy metals into Andean rivers and lakes. Low-cost bioflocculants made from local plants could help treat it with fewer chemicals.
