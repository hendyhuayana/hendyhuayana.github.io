Niño 1+2 or Niño 3.4?

Niño 1+2 tracks the ocean off northern Peru and Ecuador: it drives heavy rain, debris flows and fishery shifts along the coast. Niño 3.4 tracks the central Pacific and defines El Niño globally.

Why does +1 °C matter?

A 1 °C warmer ocean evaporates more water and reshapes winds. In 1983 and 1998, anomalies of about +4 °C brought the worst rainfall of the 20th century to Tumbes and Piura.

Who issues official alerts?

Peru's multisector commission ENFEN issues the official statements and the coastal index (ICEN). This site is educational: always follow ENFEN, SENAMHI and INDECI.
